Immersion and Storytelling is a principle that requires a compelling narrative with good flow and execution. A game that centers this is Genshin Impact, with the lore being so immersive that many people continue playing to learn more about the world of Teyvat. Each main story quest brings up new factions of power, cross-region relations, and has slowly worked towards the ultimate goal of uncovering the past of the Traveler (the protagonist) and their lost twin. Along with this, the updates add more to the story and fill in the gaps of knowledge, with lots of lore hidden in side quests, items descriptions, and even weapons. 
However, since storytelling is so subjective, it’s hard to please everyone. Some people feel overwhelmed by the amount of lore or don’t bother to learn about it because they like other things more. Storytelling is also bound to have misinterpretations, which can be tough to handle because with more complex stories, it can happen regardless of the execution. Through these challenges, with an immersive story, that alone can generate a strong, loyal audience.

Setting Clear Goals and Objectives is a principle that’s necessary to have a functioning, intuitive game. Marvel Rivals is a game that does this well. In “Domination” maps, you fight the other team to hold a point for a certain amount of time. In “Convoy” maps, you attempt to push a vehicle to its destination while fighting the defending team, where the defense is labelled as “Convergence” (because they’re… converging… against the convoy). This example demonstrates how the game has simple, yet clear objectives, with intuitive naming conventions to get the message across, making it easy for players to pick up and understand. 
If the goals and objectives aren’t clear or intuitive, there’s a chance the player will not want to play the game. There are games like Minecraft where having no clear goal tends to be the point, but in games where that isn’t the case, it tends to leave players lost and disconnected from the game, which ultimately leads to it being dropped.

Maintaining Game Flow and Player Experience is a principle necessary to keep players engaged and want to keep playing. Final Fantasy VII: Rebirth did amazing with this. The main story didn’t have much filler, so players could get through it quickly if that’s what they wanted, but they added large areas to explore, lots of mini games, and side quests in each region. This allows the player to take breaks from the somewhat heavy story while staying immersed and always having more things to do, improving their overall game experience. On an accessibility standpoint, there are different modes including a “story” mode for those who don’t want to stress too much about combat, which lets players customize their experience, and the controls are easy to pick up and recognize.
When a game doesn’t feel seamless to play, be it due to difficulty, structure of content, or controls, the player doesn’t get immersed, and they might drop it. By prioritizing game flow, it helps ensure the player will get through the game because they want to, rather than feeling like a chore.
