**One-Pager: Media Management**

**(10 points) Identify common uses of media items for games similar to yours**

I’m going to reference Espresso Tycoon for this since it’s like a much higher-level version of my game but has the same general concepts. In terms of sound, Espresso Tycoon commonly uses background music, with it differing based on the location. It also uses sound effects when walking, when adding ingredients into the coffee, and for ambience purposes (like wind blowing when you’re walking outside). There’s also voiced dialogue from the npcs you interact with. 

Aside from sound, Espresso Tycoon is pretty detailed in animations and popup art. Customers are animated walking around the restaurant and sitting down, and they can be seen talking to each other if they’re sitting at the same table. There’s popup art of the npcs when the player talks to them, along with a text box with the captions. There are also animations when adding ingredients to the coffee, showing how the drinks change and fill up gradually. The assets in general are detailed and bring a lot of beauty to the game, with detailed lighting and furniture. 

**(10 points) Identify at least two strategies to integrate such media into a game and any pros/cons**

One strategy for sound effects is to take sound snippets off the internet (there are lots of free libraries) and turn them into usable sound files for the game (.ogg files) and attach it to an object when you interact with it. The pros of this are that it’s relatively simple and most of it is just setting it up and finding a sound you like. However, it means you’re a little limited on the kinds of sounds or music that you find, so you might not get exactly what you want. 

A strategy for visual assets and animations is similar to the sound effects, which also involves using things off the internet. Itch.io has a lot of free asset packs, and anything that isn’t free is usually relatively cheap (like under $20). After downloading them, you can import them into your game and use them. Again, the pros are that it’s a lot quicker and you don’t need to draw assets yourself (though you sometimes still have to animate them, but that’s not too hard). The cons are that you’re often even more limited on what you can have, especially if your game is on a niche topic or you cannot afford to get any paid assets. You also have to be more careful when configuring the assets because some of the sprite maps have different cell sizes, so you might have to adjust the sizes to fit your game. 

An alternative to both of these would be to just make it yourself, though it’s tricky if you don’t have any experience with sound design, music, or art. It is also much more time consuming and costly if you do not have the materials or software necessary to create things yourself, but if you have the time and the skills/motivation, you can ensure you have exactly what you want for your game in the way that you envisioned it. 

**(10 points) Summarize what approach you'd use to integrate media into your game and why**

I’ve already used a lot of visual assets like sprite packs for my game during my prototype. I downloaded them off itch.io and credited them in my repository (credit the artists!) because I do not have the time to draw an entire café and player character’s worth of sprites and I found some really nice packs that were almost exactly what I was looking for. 

I’ll probably do the same for most of the sound effects, grabbing them off sound libraries, but if I have the time, I want to compose my own background music since I have experience with music composition. Video game music is really important to me, so if I can create it for my own game, I’ll be very happy.
